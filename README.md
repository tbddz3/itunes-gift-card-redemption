## How to redeem, by device

Every path starts the same way. Find the 16-digit code on the back; on some cards you have to peel or gently scratch off the label to see it.

**iPhone, in the App Store**
1. Open the App Store and tap your photo or initials.
2. Tap Redeem Code. You may be asked to sign in to your Apple Account.
3. Tap Use Camera and follow the on-screen instructions. If the camera won't cooperate, tap Enter Code Manually instead.
4. Tap Redeem.

**iPhone, in Wallet** (only available in some countries and regions, and only after you've added your Apple Account card to Wallet)
1. Open Wallet and tap your Apple Account card.
2. Tap the button, then tap Redeem.
3. Use Camera, or Enter Code Manually if scanning fails.
4. Tap Redeem.

**iPad and Apple Vision Pro** follow the iPhone App Store path exactly: App Store, tap your photo or initials, Redeem Code, scan or type, Redeem.

**Mac**
1. Open the App Store and click your name in the sidebar, or sign in to your Apple Account.
2. Click Redeem Code.
3. Click Use Camera, or Enter Code Manually if scanning fails.

**Windows PC**
1. Open the Apple Music app or the Apple TV app.
2. Click your name at the bottom of the sidebar, then click Redeem. Sign in first if prompted.
3. Follow the on-screen instructions, then click Redeem.

**A card someone emailed you:** open the email and click the "Redeem Now" button. That's the whole process.

## Why a valid code gets rejected

**You typed the serial number.** The redemption code is the 16-digit string starting with X. There's a separate serial number printed on the card back, and it looks similar enough that people enter it by mistake. It will never work.

**The label is still on.** Some cards hide the code under a peel-off or scratch-off strip. If you can't see 16 characters, you haven't found the code yet.

**The camera scan failed and you stopped there.** Use Camera is convenient, not mandatory. Enter Code Manually exists precisely for scratched, curved, or badly lit cards.

**The region doesn't match.** This is the one that costs people money. Gift cards and content codes purchased in the United States can only be linked to a US Apple Account and can only be used for purchases in the United States. ## Six rules in Apple's terms that change how you should buy

Apple's gift card terms (last updated October 14, 2024) are short and unusually consequential. The issuer is Apple Value Services, LLC, a Virginia limited liability company and an Apple Inc. affiliate.

**1. Region lock runs both ways.** A US card links only to a US account and spends only in the US. Separately, products bought online from Apple ship only within the continental US, Alaska, Hawaii, and Puerto Rico. No shipments to P.O. Box addresses, US territories, or addresses outside the United States. A gift card balance will not solve a shipping-address problem.

**2. Linking the card destroys the card.** Once a physical or digital card or code is linked to your account, the balance joins your Associated Balance and you can no longer use the card itself, including in an Apple Retail Store. Buy-to-give and buy-to-use are different purchases, and you can't reverse the decision after redeeming.

**3. The balance is not cash.** Gift cards can't be redeemed for cash, can't be returned for a cash refund except where law requires it, can't be exchanged, can't be resold, and can't be used to buy other gift cards. Unused Associated Balance isn't transferable.

**4. A credit card may still be required.** Some purchases require a card on file even when your Apple Account balance covers the full amount. A $50 balance does not guarantee a frictionless checkout.

**5. Payments don't combine.** Gift cards can't be used in combination with Apple Pay or PayPal, and can't be split across multiple credit cards. If your balance is short, you pay the remainder with a payment method on file rather than topping up the transaction with a second wallet.

**6. Closing your account requires spending the balance first.** You must spend any Associated Balance before you can close an Apple Account.

Two mechanics worth knowing: content purchases deduct from your Associated Balance first and fall back to another payment method on file if the balance is short. Purchases from the Apple online store, the Apple Store app, or by phone deduct from gift cards or codes entered at checkout first, then from your Associated Balance. Apple may also issue a virtual Apple Account card in Wallet, usable in Apple Retail Stores, if you have an iPhone or Apple Watch that supports Apple Pay.

## Discounted codes: what the below-face-value prices actually mean

Retail is simple: a $25 Apple Gift Card at a mainstream retailer costs $25.00. That's the baseline.

Third-party aggregator listings show something different. One price-comparison page, dated November 9, 2026, lists a $250 iTunes Gift Card (USA) at $219.00 and a $500 card at $430.00, which works out to roughly 12% and 14% below face value. The same page also lists a $2.06 "iTunes Gift Card" offer that doesn't correspond to any standard denomination, and a $229.99 US card whose face value isn't stated. Treat that page as a snapshot of asking prices from resellers, not as an official price list, and note that nothing in it is evidence about any specific storefront, including the one linked in this article.

The arithmetic is easy. The risk allocation is what needs reading. Apple's terms state that neither the issuer nor Apple is responsible for loss or damage from lost or stolen cards or codes, or from use without permission. For physical cards, risk of loss and title pass to the purchaser at purchase; for electronic cards, at electronic transmission to the recipient. If Apple suspects a card or code was obtained or used unlawfully, fraudulently, or in violation of the terms, it can void or deactivate it without notice and without a refund, suspend the account, and cancel orders. If a code simply doesn't function, the sole remedy is replacement of that card or code.

Put those two facts side by side and the discount has a shape: you are being paid a few percent to carry a risk that the terms place on you rather than on the seller. That doesn't make every discounted code bad. It does mean the seller's replacement policy matters more than the discount, and a seller with no stated policy is offering you a worse deal than a face-value purchase at a retailer, whatever the percentage says.

## Auto Reload and bonus codes, for anyone topping up regularly

If you subscribe to several Apple services, Auto Reload can remove the manual top-up step. You pick an amount and a trigger: weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, or whenever your balance falls below a set figure. Apple's own example: $10 monthly set to "End of Month" charges on the 28th, 29th, 30th, or 31st, whichever is the last day of that month. Set to $10 when the balance drops below $5, it charges $10 each time you cross that line.

Cancellation has a deadline. You can cancel in App Store > Account > Add Funds (labeled "Add Money" in some countries) up to one day before a scheduled reload. The charge goes to your default payment method; if that fails, another payment method on file may be charged, or the top-up simply doesn't happen. Apple can pause or disable the feature at its discretion for reasons including suspected fraud, inactivity, payment method problems, or a legal change.

Promotional bonuses attached to gift card purchases are narrower than they look. You must be an individual end user located in the United States to receive one, generally one bonus per account, and the bonus has no cash value, can't be sold or transferred, and isn't eligible for refund or exchange. Apple and participating retailers can modify or end a promotion at any time without prior notice. A "$10 bonus" is not a $10 bill.

## FAQ

**Where exactly is the code?** On the back of the card, 16 digits, usually starting with X. Peel or gently scratch the label if nothing is visible.

**Can I use a US card on a non-US Apple Account?** No. US-purchased cards and content codes link only to US accounts and spend only in the US.

**Can I buy a gift card with a gift card?** No. The terms prohibit using gift cards to purchase gift cards, and resale is prohibited too.

**Do I need a credit card to redeem?** Redeeming a code doesn't charge anything. Some purchases may still require a card on file even when the balance covers the cost.

**What if the card is lost or stolen?** Neither Apple nor the issuer is responsible, and risk of loss passed to the purchaser at the point of sale or transmission. There's no balance recovery process described in the terms.

**What if the code doesn't work at all?** The sole remedy is replacement of the card or code. Cash refunds aren't offered except where law requires them.

**How do I check a balance?** Call 1-800-MY-APPLE or use Apple's gift card balance page at apple.com/go/gcb/us. Gift cards also aren't sold in every country, so confirm availability for your region before shopping.

## Before you pay

Match the purchase to the use case:

- **Your own subscriptions, small and predictable:** buy the smallest denomination that covers a few months. There's no benefit to preloading a large balance you'll spend slowly.
- **A gift:** an Apple Gift Card is the flexible choice, but confirm the recipient's Apple Account country first. A US card sent to a non-US account is a wasted purchase.
- **Cross-border or "my account isn't local":** settle the region question before anything else. This is the single largest source of dead codes, and it isn't fixable after the fact.
- **Chasing a discount:** compare the discount against the seller's replacement terms. If a code arrives dead and the seller won't replace it, you've paid for nothing, and Apple's terms won't help you.

Two things to confirm at checkout, every time: the card's region matches your Apple Account's country, and the seller states what happens if the code doesn't redeem. Everything else is secondary.
